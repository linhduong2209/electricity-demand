In this study, by using the capabilities of the CatBoost model and meta-heuristic algorithms, as
well as the hybridization technique, an attempt was made to improve the prediction of electricity
demand on a short-term scale. For this purpose, the hybrid CatBoost-PPSO model was suggested
in this study to predict electricity demand based on weather variables. Finally, by conducting a
case study and comparing the results of the proposed model with five other hybrid models, the
results were evaluated and compared using various statistical indexes. The general approach used
in this study is that the hyper-parameters of the Catboost were optimized using a meta-heuristic
algorithm, and the best of them were used during the forecasting process. Also, during the
network training, the K-Fold cross-validation algorithm is used to avoid over-fitting. The evalu­
ation results of the models based on the test data showed that the hybrid CatBoost-PPSO model
has high capabilities in short-term electricity demand forecasting. The indices obtained from this
model show better values than other hybrid models. For example, the RMSE value of this model is
equal to 42.3, which shows an improvement of almost 9.5% compared to the hybrid CatBoostALO model.

1. Introduction
According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), the world’s highest energy consumer countries in 2021 were China, the United
States, India, Russia, and Japan. These five countries alone accounted for around 55% of the world’s total energy consumption, while
the top ten highest energy-consuming countries accounted for around 62% of global energy consumption [1].
The IEA notes that the high energy consumption in these countries is primarily driven by factors such as population growth,
economic development, industrialization, lifestyle choices, and energy policies [2]. The increasing energy consumption in these
countries also highlights the need for greater energy efficiency and the development of cleaner and more sustainable energy sources to
mitigate the impacts of climate change [3].
The simplicity of converting electrical energy into other energies and its quick transfer to different places has increased the
importance of its use in human life so that in today’s life, electricity is considered as one of the most important sources of energy
supply. Electricity consumption is one of the basic criteria for improving the welfare of nations. These advantages, along with other
factors such as rapid population growth, urbanization development, increase in the level of welfare and life, and industrial and
commercial development have caused the scope of electricity consumption in countries to be expanded [4].
The limitation of energy sources and the increase of environmental pollution, as well as the increasing warming of the earth, on the
one hand, and the increase in energy consumption costs, on the other hand, have caused people and organizations to think about the
management of energy consumption [5]. If the amount of electricity production exceeds the requirement due to its uneconomical
storage, the electricity industry will suffer many losses. Therefore, the correct forecast of electricity consumption will manage the
electricity production capacities and prevent possible future losses. An essential prerequisite to studying and managing an energy
system, as previously mentioned, is good demand forecasting, which is needed not only for efficient investment in capacity expansion
but also has an effective role in monitoring environmental issues, and study plans [6].
In the following, the literature review of the research topic is examined. The literature review tried to refer more to the models
presented by researchers in predicting electricity consumption. Torrini et al. [7] used the independent variable of GDP to forecast
electricity consumption by the fuzzy logic approach. They compared their model with official predictions and the Holt model. The
results showed that the fuzzy model has less error than the Holt model, and the results of this model were very promising according to
the official predictions. Al-Musaylh et al. [8] used Multivariate Adaptive Regression Spline (MARS), Support Vector Regression (SVR),
and Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) models to forecast electricity demand in Queensland, Australia. The results
showed that the MARS and SVR models could be more suitable compared to the ARIMA model for short-term prediction of electricity
demand. Tondolo de Miranda et al. [9] used an artificial neural network (ANN) and fuzzy logic to predict electric load in the context of
demand management. The results showed that this system could provide estimates of electricity demand with more satisfactory results.
Schirmer et al. [10] evaluated the performance of different regression methods, including Linear Regression, Decision Trees, Deep
Neural Networks, Recurrent Neural Networks, Gated Recurrent Units and Long Short Time Memory (LSTM) to predict the consumption
of electrical energy in residential buildings. The results showed that the LSTM regression model outperformed all other regression
algorithms and improved the predictive accuracy.
Li et al. [11] used cluster analysis, cubist regression models, and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) to predict the total electricity
consumption and the peak electricity demand in the university building. The results showed that the proposed strategy could improve
prediction accuracy by 42.2%. In a study, Shadkam [12] used SARIMAX to predict average daily electricity consumption and peak
demand in two university buildings. The results showed that this model could be used to make informed decisions about identifying
anomalies in electricity consumption trends and quantifying energy and cost-saving measures. Zhang et al. [13] used a hybrid model,
including K-means clustering and the CatBoost model, to predict the electric load and energy management of electric power systems.
The results showed that the proposed model is more accurate than other ARMA, LSTM, GBDT, and CatBoost models in predicting
electricity consumption.
Zhang and Guo [14] used a hybrid method based on support vector regression (SVR) to forecast future electricity demand effec­
tively. The results showed that the proposed method could predict better than other standard and advanced methods. Khan et al. [15]
used a hybrid model based on machine learning (ML) techniques, which is a combination of three ML algorithms (extreme gradient

1.1. Main novelties and contributions
Energy consumption is an important issue of global concern. Accurate energy consumption forecasting can help balance energy
demand and energy production. Data-driven energy consumption forecasting methods, which predict the short-term future energy
consumption values based on historical data, are important elements in building information modeling. The short-term and very shortterm energy consumption forecasting techniques are useful for residential energy demand-side management, electricity price market
design, energy efficiency and maintenance scheduling of large-scale complex smart power grids. The literature review shows that
researchers proposed different methods to improve the accuracy of energy consumption prediction. Each method has its characteristics
that are used according to different conditions. However, the discrepancy between actual and predicted energy consumption is still
challenging. Electricity consumption demand can vary even hourly, so it is very important to use short-term forecasting models that
can accurately predict. Since the CatBoost model usually does not consider time series information, using this model can be a good
option to solve this problem. The CatBoost model is a new approach with proven capabilities in various studies. Therefore, in this
study, a hybrid model was presented by combining the CatBoost model with Phasor Particle Swarm Optimization (PPSO) metaheuristic algorithm to improve the prediction of short-term electricity consumption.

2. Methodology
The main purpose of this study is to provide a model to improve the short-term forecast of electricity demand based on weather
variables. Since the period of this study is short-term and, on the other hand, the number of research variables is also high, the
forecasting process will be difficult. Therefore, it can be useful to apply methods based on machine learning. In this study, by
combining categorical boosting (CatBoost) and Phasor Particle Swarm Optimization (PPSO) algorithm, a hybrid model was presented
to improve forecasts. This model is called the "hybrid CatBoost-PPSO model." Catboost is a machine learning model developed by
Yandex researchers. Since this algorithm has recently been open source, its applications and scope in various sciences are increasing
significantly. The most important feature and advantage of this model compared to other ML models is that it can work on different
data formats, which has increased its efficiency. Also, this model can be integrated with other deep learning models, which is useful in
creating commercial applications. Catboost is used to handle regression and classification problems [18].
The general approach applied in this study is that the CatBoost model was used as the main modeling method; then, the PPSO
algorithm was used to optimize the hyper-parameters of the CatBoost. Also, to prevent overfitting, the K-Fold cross-validation method
was used during the network training process. In most machine learning problems, the model’s parameters must be estimated. If the
number of parameters is large, the complexity of the model increases and calculations may not be easily performed. Cross-validation is
an effective method for estimating the number of model parameters [19,20]. In the models based on machine learning, overfitting is
"the production of an analysis that corresponds too closely or exactly to a particular set of data, and may therefore fail to fit to
additional data or predict future observations reliably". The solution for such a problem can be using the cross-validation method, in
which the goal is to determine the number of suitable variables (parameters) of the model. For this purpose, the K-Fold cross-validation
method was used in this study.
To check and compare the accuracy of the proposed model, the results were compared with the other five hybrid models. The
optimizers of these models are Fruit-fly Optimization Algorithm (FOA), Satin Bowerbird Optimization (SBO), Battle Royale Optimi­
zation (BRO), Moth Flame Optimization (MFO), and Ant Lion Optimizer (ALO). Also, the statistical evaluation indices used for
comparing the models are RMSE, MAPE, MAE, RAE, R2, and WI, which are mentioned based on the following equations [21–24]:

2.1. The hybrid CatBoost-PPSO model
In this section, the introduction of the hybrid CatBoost-PPSO model is discussed in more detail. The flowchart of this model is
shown in Fig. 1.
According to Fig. 1, the model starts with implementing the CatBoost model. First, by determining the test and training dataset, the
number of samples and the features are determined. Then, categorical features will be handled using the target statistics (TS) method.
A categorical feature contains a set of categories that have different characteristics and cannot be compared with each other. The

advantage of the TS method is that it handles the categorical features with minimal lack of information [25].
In the next step, the categorical features are combined. Features can also be of different types (numeric, string, etc.), so they must be
handled in a specific way. One of the most important advantages of combining the categorical features is that it can identify strong
dependencies. Since by increasing the number of categorical features in the data set, the number of their different combinations in­
creases exponentially, so Catboost uses this technique that, for each tree, combines all categorical features and their combinations used
in the previous split of the same tree with categorical features in the dataset [26]. Then, T regression trees (CART) will be built one after
another. Finally, the values of predictors will be averaged, providing a more accurate prediction of the model [27]. Also, during the
network training, the K-Fold cross-validation algorithm is used to prevent overfitting. If the training dataset is randomly divided into K
subsamples or folds with the same volume at each step of the cross-validation process, the K-1 number of these folds can be used as the
training dataset, and one is considered as the testing dataset. By repeating the algorithm K times, every fold gets to be in a testing set
exactly once and gets to be in a training dataset k-1 times. Finally, the average of the results is used to evaluate the model’s perfor­
mance [28].
Every time the loop of the Catboost repeats, if the stopping condition (i.e., the maximum number of iterations) allows, the PPSO
algorithm is executed, and the hyper-parameter values are optimized. The PPSO algorithm is an extended version of the Particle Swarm
algorithm, which is based on the Phasor theory. For this purpose, an initial population is randomly produced at first. In this algorithm,

2.3. Research variables
In this study, the electricity demand prediction is investigated based on weather variables. To this end, the electricity demand in kW
is considered as the output variable (dependent variable). The input variables (independent variables) also include air pressure
[mmHg], air temperature [◦ C], relative humidity [%], solar irradiation [W/mA2], and wind speed [m/s]. To conduct a case study, an
actual dataset is used which is based on the electricity consumed by a residential customer at a predetermined location. The dataset is
gathered between December 2010 and November 2018 with a 1-h time-step resolution.

6. Conclusion
The precise prediction of electricity demand in the short-term is crucial for effective power system planning, which involves tasks
such as scheduling the use of power generating capacity, purchasing fuel, and managing the power system. This is particularly
16

Journal of Building Engineering 71 (2023) 106432

important for the residential sector, which is a major contributor to peak loads in most electrical systems. In this study, the hybrid
Catboost-PPSO model was investigated to improve the short-term forecast of electricity demand based on weather variables. The
general approach in this study was that the hyper-parameters of the CatBoost were optimized with the help of the PPSO algorithm.
These optimal values were used to train the network. Finally, to evaluate the accuracy of the proposed model by conducting a case
study, the results were compared with five other hybrid models using different evaluation indices. The results of this study proved that
the approach used in this research has a significant effect on improving the predictions. The results showed that the proposed model is
highly accurate in predicting short-term electricity demand. In general, the examination of various evaluation indices showed that the
PPSO, SBO, FOA, BRO, MFO, and ALO algorithms are the most accurate in improving the results and reducing the error, respectively.
The main results of the are described as follows.
• According to the obtained results, in the test data, the lowest RMSE and RAE is obtained for the proposed Catboost-PPSO model by
42.03 and 0.0911.
• Checking the accuracy of trained hybrid models based on the test dataset showed that the hybrid Catboost-ALO model has the
weakest performance in predicting electricity demand. For example, the evaluation of the models based on the test dataset shows
that the R2 value of the hybrid Catboost-PPSO model and the hybrid Catboost-ALO model are respectively equal to 0.9709303 and
0.964482, which are the highest and lowest values of this index among all hybrid models.
• Although the PPSO technique may have lower accuracy when training data is compared to other methods, it performs better when
it comes to testing data which is more crucial in such models.
• Additionally, the PPSO method outperforms all other methods in terms of convergence speed, which is a significant advantage for
optimization methods.
Accurate prediction of electricity consumption is essential for providing actionable insights to decision-makers for managing
volume and potential trends in future energy consumption for efficient resource management. One crucial issue that needs attention in
load forecasting is the data requirements of each region, which pose a limitation to deep learning applications. Many operational
regions lack meteorological stations, and if they exist, the available data may be affected by data privacy concerns. To enhance
forecasting procedures in data-driven power system applications, it is necessary to address these challenges and develop models that
can be applied to regions with limited data. Therefore, future studies could focus on introducing and enhancing models that can be
generalized to regions with data shortages.
